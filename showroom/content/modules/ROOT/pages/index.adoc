= Virtualization + AI 101 — Trace a VM to Governed AI
:navtitle: Welcome

== The decision

A virtual machine and an AI service can share an OpenShift cluster without
proving that the application inside the VM actually reached a model. In this
lab you will follow one request from a VM-hosted workload through OpenShift
platform services to governed Intel Xeon CPU inference and back to a human
operator.

This is a *101 trace-and-explain lab*. You inspect an established contract and
runtime. The future 201 lab is separate: there, the learner must author the
VM-to-AI contract, configure identity and networking, test denial and failure,
and prove cleanup.

== Learner outcome

At the end you can:

* identify the VM/VMI, Service, adapter, model, validation, and authority boundaries;
* prove that a request originated inside the VM;
* distinguish platform connectivity from AI participation;
* compare a healthy request with an unavailable-model condition;
* export a claim-to-evidence map for review.

== Journey

[cols="1,3,1",options="header"]
|===
| Step | Evidence question | Time
| Verify | Which runtime objects are present and ready? | 10 min
| Trace | Where are the network, inference, and authority boundaries? | 10 min
| Invoke | Did the request originate inside the VM? | 15 min
| Inspect | Which model ran and what deterministic checks accepted its output? | 15 min
| Failure | Does the same path fail closed without inventing an AI result? | 10 min
| Evidence | Can you state exactly what was and was not proven? | 15 min
| Cleanup | Did you remove the learner credential and temporary receipts without deleting the lab? | 5 min
|===

No cost, performance, or capacity result is pre-authored in this lab. A
`REHEARSAL` or `OFFLINE` response demonstrates mechanics only; it is not live
inference proof.
